01 · Bottleneck

Why deadstock stays stuck

Small brands often have useful surplus, but direct barter requires two firms to want exactly what the other has at the same time.

01
Double coincidence of wants

A has denim and wants viscose. B has viscose, but wants jersey. A↔B fails even though both lots are useful.

02
Standardization is expensive

A universal deadstock grade needs agreement on quality, defects, age, testing and enforcement across many firms.

03 · Reframe
Use revealed acceptance instead

Ask each participant: “Which lots would you accept for your surplus?” Preference becomes the practical quality filter.

03 · Mechanism

From preference to cycle

The mechanism borrows the logic of kidney-exchange cycles: incompatible pairs can become compatible as a group.

1. List surplusMaterial, composition, GSM, quantity, condition, location.
2. State acceptance setDefine which incoming lots are acceptable instead of relying on one universal grade.
3. Build directed graphAn edge means “this company would accept that company’s lot.”
4. Find cyclesSearch 2-, 3- and 4-way loops with no company repeated.
5. Check & reserveVerify economics and minimum gain, approve, then reserve lots to prevent double commitment.
Economic filter

Every participant must benefit

Net Gain = W − S − C

W = value of incoming wanted lot · S = clearance value of surplus given up · C = logistics / transaction cost. A cycle is recommended only when each participant’s gain is ≥ 0 and clears its own minimum hurdle.

Research design

What the project tests

Dataset

15–20 participating firms and roughly 20–30 deadstock lots with self-declared acceptance sets.

Key comparison

Compare direct 2-way matching with 2–4-way cycle matching: matched lots, match rate and the participant threshold where cycles become useful.

Limitations

Stated preferences may differ from real purchasing behavior; adverse selection, inspection, shipping and trust costs still matter.

Prototype data are fictional and are used only to demonstrate the mechanism. Demo figures should not be interpreted as empirical market results.
